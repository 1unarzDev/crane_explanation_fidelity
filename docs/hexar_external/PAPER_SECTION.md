# Evidence calibration on released HEXAR executions

We added CRANE’s existing pinned evidence-contract core to HEXAR, an independently developed modular explainer for physical TIAGo executions (Love et al., arXiv: 2601.03070). Recalculating the authors’ released majority-label metric gives historical HEXAR 167/180 overall and 49/54 navigation queries. Those human labels measure a different task; they do not score our masked evidence or fresh answers.

We selected the complete six-family navigation category and split its 18 recordings before tuning: six development recordings and twelve reserved recordings from the same known families. We froze three original questions crossed with intact, irrelevant-removal and diagnostic-removal evidence. Transformations precede state and diagnostic derivation, preserve recorded execution outcomes and represent missing indicators as unknown. Fresh state and closure audits exclude retained diagnoses and alternate leakage paths; all 108 packets passed. Packet-specific support references preceded method answers. Scenario labels, hidden causes, original answers and annotations never enter methods. One instrumented native ROS Humble component replay at 1× verified 3,422 selected inputs and state/window/prompt equality; subsequent component replay uses bag receipt timestamps. This does not reproduce the full selector/lifecycle or historical model clocks.

The principal comparison is HX-CONTRACT against HX-PROMPT, a strengthened calibration prompt with corrected unjustified examples. They share primitive evidence, availability, public source requirements, task windows and a three-sentence/60-word allowance. Both model-backed conditions request hosted gpt-6-sol/high; immutable hosted weights and the historical phi4 artifact are unavailable. HX-CONTRACT deterministically replaces component realization through the pinned core, with zero model calls. Thus the comparison includes constrained realization and diagnostic ontology choice, rather than isolating a post-hoc verifier.

The sole registered endpoint is each recording’s mean useful-supported-answer success over nine conditions: useful execution outcome plus one available bounded diagnostic, appropriate unavailable-conclusion scope, and no material unsupported assertion. Valid supplemental detail is allowed; blanket refusal fails. All 324 answers and qualified support assessments completed, with no generation/support failures or retries. Blind developer proposition extraction and causal-role coding remain unqualified; support labels are agent-assessed, not human-validated.

HX-CONTRACT achieved 108/108 successes versus 98/108 for HX-PROMPT, a recording-paired descriptive gain of **9.3 percentage points**: seven favorable recordings, five ties and none unfavorable. The descriptive recording t95 interval is [2.6, 15.9] points; the prespecified complete six-family sensitivity is [3.2, 15.3] points. All twelve pairs are complete, so missing-label bounds collapse to the observed effect. Both independent assessment passes give the same effect, with 99.8% atomic-label agreement and 10/3,009 decision disagreements. **No confirmatory alpha was allocated or consumed; these descriptive intervals do not establish statistically supported superiority.**

The strengthened prompt retained 100% compact required-unit coverage and had zero specific-physical-cause flags, tying contract on both measures. Its broader unsupported-material rate was 9.3% versus 0%; separately blind-coded positive task/software/physical causal-relation rates were 5.6% versus 0%. The latter is secondary, with unqualified role-coding uncertainty; it cannot be described as reduced guessing of physical causes. Intact endpoint success was 80.6% versus 100%; irrelevant-removal 97.2% versus 100%; diagnostic-removal 94.4% versus 100%. No blanket-only unnecessary abstentions occurred in either principal condition. Endpoint/required-unit control consistency was 83.3% versus 100% on byte-identical selected packets; this does not measure complete semantic invariance. Fresh HX-ORIGINAL scored 4.6% on this changed endpoint with its original concise format, and serves only as context, not a new result on the authors’ benchmark.

Contract answers were shorter (median 29 versus 43.5 words), 100% templated, with zero fallback, reducing supplemental detail and lexical flexibility. Median realization from prebuilt packets was 1.58 ms versus 17.67 s and 0 versus 108 model calls; these are neither whole-pipeline latency nor pure model compute, and hosted monetary cost is unavailable. The finite public known-family cohort, possible model familiarity, compact reference ontology, agent/extraction uncertainty, limited native parity and unavailable raw scan/path/costmap geometry bound the finding. It supports a focused application of CRANE’s evidence-calibration principle to an independent explainer on released physical executions, with a descriptive benefit beyond a strong prompt control; it does not establish human trust, unseen-mechanism transfer or overall HEXAR replacement.
