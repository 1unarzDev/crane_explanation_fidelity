# Sample size remains unset

Neither N=72 nor N=96 is frozen. The former is a conditional McNemar planning calculation; the latter is a paired e-test example at λ=.4. Final population, acquisition independence, realized variability and technical invalidity must be qualified before selecting the final procedure, meaningful effect regime, N and reserves. Development-only paired outputs may inform those assumptions; none enters confirmation.

The `family_power_report.json` runs 40,000 seeded draws for each of 120 combinations of N, family regime and invalidity. All draws operate at the independent episode level, not answer level. For the e-test example at N=96, conditional power is .917 under homogeneous discordance=.50/favorable=.80, .677 under adverse family covariation, .412 under degraded direction, and .00095 under the heterogeneous average-null regime. Monte Carlo maximum standard error is .0025. These are hypothetical scenarios, not measured properties of the new simulator.

At 10% technical invalidity, four reserve attempts per family give about .77 probability of filling N=96. Therefore that reserve is not automatically adequate. Its final size must reflect acquisition qualification and a prospectively accepted completion probability; no answer or judge result may influence technical replacement. Exhaustion does not authorize enlarging the cohort.

The question battery is clustered within each episode and changes whole-episode discordance probabilities. Existing `power_report.json` includes shared-shock battery dependence sensitivity, but its nested-error scenario does not establish zero unfavorable discordance. Final whole-episode paired planning must include both discordant directions and realistic degradation from the inspected development score. The old +9.3-point answer effect and 7:0 favorable split cannot be equated to a new population risk reduction.

Final N/reserve fields remain null until those scientific and engineering steps are complete. This deliberately prevents a planning example from silently becoming the fixed-N protocol.

## Two-direction battery and exact reserve sensitivity

`battery_reserve_power_report.json` adds 80 hypothetical Gaussian-copula regimes, each with 80,000 independent episodes and nine answers per method. It varies contract answer failure from .04 to .12 against prompt failure .10, within-method latent correlation from zero to one, and cross-method correlation from zero to one. Unlike the earlier nested-error illustration, imperfectly correlated methods can fail in either discordant direction. No actual semantic outcomes are loaded. Its exact homogeneous e-test power uses the simulated paired probabilities, with Monte Carlo uncertainty in those probabilities; it does not replace the separate family-heterogeneity report.

For example, at answer failure .04 versus .10 and cross-method latent correlation .5, N=96 planning power decreases from .952 with independent answers within a method to .574 at within-method correlation .5 and .148 at .9. Correlation one makes all nine answers one event and gives approximately .005 power in that regime. Thus an answer-level benefit does not by itself justify 72 or 96 whole-episode pairs. Smaller advantages can also be poorly matched to the candidate fixed fraction .4; fraction and minimum meaningful effect remain prospective design choices, never parameters tuned to confirmation.

The new report enumerates technical completion exactly rather than relying only on simulated draws. With 16 valid episodes per family, 10% independent technical invalidity and four reserves per family, full-cohort completion probability is .767355. Six reserves reach 95% and seven reach 99% under those assumptions. These numbers do not establish the actual invalidity rate: few successful engineering runs cannot justify assuming a zero rate, and rate uncertainty and family differences must inform the final reserve. The implementation and its report preserve null final-N/reserve decisions.
