# Independent action-rejection validity review — development only

Zero late-command rejection is not a necessary scientific condition for an explanation study. A command rejected correctly before actuator mutation can be a faithfully observed execution fault. Explanation validity requires trustworthy episode identity, observations, execution and exported evidence; benchmark latency cleanliness answers a different question. This judgment concerns a prospective successor population only. Existing gates, failed dispositions and scientific counts remain unchanged, and no explanation outcomes were read.

The reviewed gate rejects cross-episode, duplicate/out-of-order and bounded-lag receipts before recording acceptance. Its age calculation uses sourceObservationTick when available and receiveTick otherwise. Therefore a stale rejection establishes violation of the configured provenance-age policy, not transport residence, Nav2 input consumption, physical causation or even an observation-origin delay when provenance is unknown. Future ticks and source-clock mapping must be independently validated: a subtraction passing a maximum-lag check alone does not establish temporal correctness.

Trace-qualified admission is defensible if declared before fresh acquisition and applied without explanation-score knowledge. Count both successful and unsuccessful navigation outcomes from the prospectively defined configuration population. Preserve each selected identity, including technically invalid acquisitions, under a declared handling/replacement rule; do not choose retries by whether a run is clean or diagnostically interesting. A rule that admits recorded execution faults changes the operational population and requires a new documented platform/collector/evidence version, even if physics and controller commands are unchanged. Its results cannot silently replace the old zero-rejection population.

Receipt conservation must use unique receipt identities and terminal dispositions, with explicit episode-boundary accounting. Received equals overwritten plus cleared plus pending plus consumed only if these are disjoint exhaustive receipt states. Accepted plus rejected equals gate attempts only if every attempt is audited; it does not necessarily equal consumed receipts if one receipt can be retried. Track repeated gate attempts separately. Confirm payload/sequence/source identity, receipt and application ticks, configured policy and maximum lag for every decision, plus actuator acceptance/application linkage. Reconcile independently with worker counters, writer completion, audit loss/error counters and mailbox-reset behavior. Unknown provenance is a recorded limitation; do not infer an observation-to-action chain from receive time alone.

Keep independent observation integrity, clock mapping, episode/namespace identity, runtime error, sensing, depth-buffer and water validity requirements. Actual cross-episode application, accepted stale commands under the active policy, corrupt observations or incomplete receipt accounting remain invalid. A correctly rejected old-episode receipt might also be observable behavior in a different expressly defined study, but this review does not propose expanding that scope. B2 and B4 must receive identical available receipt facts if those facts become robot-visible evidence; annotation must distinguish supported rejection facts from unsupported cause stories. Hidden instrumentation can establish acquisition validity without being exposed as diagnostic evidence, but it cannot justify judging a method against inaccessible required facts.

Before activating a successor, validate the trace machinery with predetermined synthetic rejection cases, receive/overwrite/reset races and controlled clock errors. Verify accepted actions have permissible age and episode identity, rejected payloads never reach actuation, and injected losses prevent admission. Compare instrumented and original workloads under fixed conditions without explanation scores to measure overhead and behavior changes. Instrumentation equivalence is a hypothesis until measured; audit logging can itself create delay.

Report prospective fault incidence by scenario family, provenance availability, lag and rejection reason. Freeze admission independently of winning method. A zero-rejection subset is useful as a labelled sensitivity analysis; it is a different conditional population and cannot replace an unfavorable primary result. Predefined scenario strata and a fixed allocation can reduce noise. Post hoc filtering to the most diagnostic faults cannot establish general superiority. The eventual confirmatory protocol must state whether it targets the operational mix including trace-qualified execution faults or the clean-latency conditional population, and plan N accordingly.

Useful development hypotheses: complete traces will separate valid controller/transport failures from corrupted acquisitions; rejection incidence will vary with workload and approach family; legitimate rejection facts may improve diagnostic measurement reliability; instrumentation may alter timing. These hypotheses require fresh independent measurements. No evidence here establishes their truth or a B4-vs-B2 effect.
