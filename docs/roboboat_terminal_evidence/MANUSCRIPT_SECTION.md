# Integration-ready marine development section

## Navigation success versus sustained docking

We study whether temporal evidence contracts improve useful, correct explanations of autonomous surface-vessel docking compared with an equally informed repository/tool agent. The marine setting matters because measured motion can continue after navigation reports success, whereas docking requires compliance over a declared interval. We reuse the validated simulator, controller, docking environment and immutable player. The contribution is an auditable completion certificate and evidence-sufficiency evaluation, rather than a new autonomy stack or identified disturbance model.

The public independent task fixes position error at at most 0.40 m, heading error at at most 0.35 rad, translational speed at at most 0.05 m/s and yaw rate at at most 0.05 rad/s, with an oriented hull/berth condition and prohibited-contact restrictions. The separately declared development contract evaluates five seconds beginning at the first post-result odometry observation, with eight seconds captured. This sampled contract does not replace the existing continuous docking evaluator. A valid point violation establishes failure; missing conditions establish unknown. Complete sampled compliance requires declared capture coverage and all required measurements. Without justified intersample bounds it cannot establish continuous compliance. Contact absence requires aligned complete event coverage, rather than silence in an unspecified stream.

The certificate distinguishes reported action outcome, instantaneous conditions, subsequent motion, interval-qualified completion and physical cause. It retains action/result identity, event/measurement provenance, effective governing configuration, signed margins, violation witnesses, sample gaps and unavailable components. Body-frame measured velocity is rotated into the stable odom frame, and heading error is wrapped. Command speed is not substituted for measured speed; traveled distance is not added to radial position error. An independent implementation checks arithmetic and temporal references.

A retained historical development example reported navigation success with result-adjacent position error 0.3738 m and speed 0.0486 m/s, leaving only 0.0262 m position margin. At 239.040 s a post-result sample had error 0.4004 m, exceeding the 0.4000 m requirement within the declared dwell. The eight-second endpoint had error 0.5614 m. This establishes a positional violation, while leaving its physical cause unidentified. The historical recording lacks an exact action-return event time; its first post-result observation is not relabeled as that event. Prospective captures retain actual result-receipt identity and time. Historical replay adds no new confirmation data.

Each physical recording supplies nested removal-only evidence levels: L0 action status and public task/configuration; L1 result-adjacent pose and measured velocity; L2 post-result trajectory and timing. The same question asks whether the specified docking task completed and what evidence explains any difference from navigation success. We remove derived outcomes, endpoint summaries, evaluator labels and access paths that reconstruct withheld observations. Genuine useful robot-visible actuation/contact telemetry is unavailable, so L3 is deferred. Construction-defined contact evidence used for semantic qualification is explicitly distinct from physical robot telemetry.

Fresh development comprises seven valid recordings in three approach clusters, including two complete original internal-XY-tolerance pairs. A retained strict capture failure is excluded under the declared validity rule. Two later fixed stopped-threshold variants remain in the same clusters and add no independent configuration N. The original records exhibit position, speed or yaw-rate violations despite reported navigation success. The later variants establish sampled kinematic/hull compliance over the fixed dwell, with full docking unknown because contact remains unavailable. One longer capture crosses the position bound after that dwell, demonstrating why interval scope matters. No environment, baseline controller or physics change was made; experiment-specific overrides are separately versioned.

The strong B2 agent receives the same public contract, primitive observations, effective YAML and executable certificate/renderer dependencies as deterministic B4. It may use scripts or call the renderer. Both original complete configuration pairs tie under the development score. A separate contact-policy-v2 replay over two inspected settling records produces twelve responses, with complete project-reviewed claim inventories and two-pass support judgments plus disagreement-only adjudication. Both methods communicate all 24 common required units and both answerable sampled-compliance intervals. Strict development success is 5/6 for B2 and 6/6 for B4. The sole B2 failure is a 20 ms growth-reference mismatch corresponding to approximately 0.000145 m; it changes no supported task outcome, compliance witness or information coverage. We therefore do not interpret this strict-score difference as material useful-outcome superiority. Supplemental detail and language flexibility remain relevant tradeoffs.

Semantic labels are agent-assessed using current qualified bindings and bounded marine extensions, not human validation. Same-family assessment may share errors. Earlier failed qualification suites, alternate citation spans and operational failures remain retained with their original dispositions. A prospective materiality/citation extension distinguishes outcome-changing errors from minor supplemental discrepancies; it does not rescore inspected banks or authorize inference. Its exact qualification disposition is reported separately in STATUS.md and the bound artifacts.

There are no boat confirmation or replication observations and no allocated or consumed marine alpha. Seven recordings, evidence levels and repeated judge passes are not seven independent paired configurations. No comparative confidence interval or significance claim is established. Confirmation requires explicit program-ledger coordination, resources, qualified endpoint measurement, an untouched configuration registry and approved clustered sequential analysis. This scoped development result supports the usefulness and auditability of the certificate/benchmark; comparative superiority remains an empirical question.

## Related work and limitations

Docking-policy explanations using linear model trees already exist (Gjærum et al., JMSE 2021; arXiv:2203.00368). Contrastive maritime avoidance explanations already use planner costs and physical measures (Jose et al., arXiv:2604.08032). CORALL, AIS-LLM and maritime scenario/risk-narrative work establish broader explained navigation and numerical-to-language directions. Our narrow target is interval-qualified physical completion and evidence sufficiency, rather than novelty of templates, runtime verification or natural-language maritime interfaces. RELATED_WORK.md records inspected source scope; an abstract is not an implementation audit.

Results concern simulator observations in a limited approach population. There is no hardware validation, justified continuous-time proof or genuinely measured contact-free full docking success. Drift alone identifies neither waves, current nor wind; a marine craft model supplies interpretation, not episode-specific causal identification. Deterministic final language constrains flexibility. Annotation uncertainty, low independent N and tied useful-outcome performance prevent broad superiority claims. Marine and land results remain separate; no post-hoc pooling is proposed.
