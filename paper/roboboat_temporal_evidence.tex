\subsection{Temporal evidence in simulated surface navigation}
\label{sec:marine-temporal-evidence}

We apply the same evidence-contract principle to simulated surface navigation,
where navigation-software success and interval-qualified physical task completion
are distinct propositions. Removal-only evidence conditions distinguish reported
status, instantaneous measurements, and sampled post-result behavior, preserving
unknown conditions rather than upgrading them into successful docking. Marine-specific
adapters and computations implement the paper's truth-versus-support distinction;
this is not zero-shot transfer of an unchanged land implementation.

The application contract fixes a five-second dwell beginning at the first delivered
post-result odometry observation. Requirements are position error at most 0.40~m,
wrapped heading error at most 0.35~rad, measured translational speed at most
0.05~m/s, absolute yaw rate at most 0.05~rad/s, an oriented hull within a declared
berth, and absence of prohibited contact. Nav2's stopped goal checker evaluates
pose and instantaneous velocity conditions; action success does not certify this
additional dwell or contact requirement~\cite{nav2controller}. A mismatch with the
stricter application contract is not evidence of a navigation-stack bug.

We fixed the entire closed development cohort before independently recomputing
descriptive findings from raw delivered odometry and declared contracts
(Table~\ref{tab:marine-temporal-cohort}). Its 48 attempts comprise 24 geometry draws
with two tolerance variants each; 22 pairs are fully admitted. All 46 admitted
recordings contain an identified action-success receipt. Forty-five cover the
sampled dwell under the declared 0.06~s maximum-gap rule. Twenty-three contain a
required-condition violation inside the dwell; 22 satisfy observed kinematic/hull
checks over the sampled interval but lack contact coverage; one has incomplete
coverage and no observed violation. Contact is unknown throughout. These are
retained-development counts, not population prevalence, a navigation-error rate,
or explanation accuracy.

Figure~\ref{fig:marine-temporal-evidence} links one timeline to its evidence ladder.
The result-adjacent position error is 0.3982~m and measured speed is 0.0438~m/s.
A within-dwell sample at simulator time 284.7148~s has error 0.400264~m, exceeding
the bound. L0 supports reported success; L1 adds bounded adjacent measurements;
L2 supplies an interval-violation witness. The plot anchors at the first post-result
observation, not an invented simulator timestamp for the client receipt. Sampled
satisfaction cannot prove continuous compliance without intersample bounds.
Motion does not uniquely identify waves, current, wind, or actuator
defects~\cite{fossen2021handbook}.

Separate inspected B2/B4 comparisons tied useful coverage: both communicated all
24 common units and both answerable sampled-compliance intervals. Strict 5/6 versus
6/6 contact-replay scores hinge on a roughly 0.000145~m temporal-reference discrepancy
and do not establish material superiority. Recent deterministic-output checks establish
numerical/contract conformance, not independently judged language correctness.
Existing docking-policy explanations use surrogate models~\cite{gjarum2021docking};
our case concerns support for execution completion. It establishes a reproducible
application of the evidence-calibration principle and descriptive simulator behavior,
while comparative effectiveness, continuous-time proof, and hardware transfer remain
unestablished.
